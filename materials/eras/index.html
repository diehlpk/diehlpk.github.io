Four Eras of Asynchronous Many-Task Systems

An interactive view of the data behind the paper Four Eras of Asynchronous Many-Task Systems: the foundational papers of AMT systems (the seeds), the works that cite them and how the systems depend on each other. Hover or tap any mark for details; tap a seed to open its profile.

06

Mechanisms studied per system

Which mechanisms has the literature studied for which system? Each cell counts the papers of the curated corpus ( papers on task-based runtimes) whose system field names the system and whose themes include the mechanism; a paper can count for several systems and mechanisms. The matrix shows what the literature studies, not which features a system officially supports. Tap a cell or a system to open the list of papers with links.

07

Direct citations among the seeds

A filled cell means that the seed of its row cites the seed of its column (OpenAlex reference lists); both are ordered by year. Hover or tap a name to highlight a seed's references and citers.

Swipe the matrix sideways; tap a name to highlight it.

08

Co-citation network

Seeds placed by how often works cite them together (multidimensional scaling of Salton's cosine). Drag nodes; tap one to highlight its partners, double-tap to open its profile.

Swipe sideways to see the whole network.

    Community venues and events

    Workshops, conferences, user meetings, BoFs and tutorials dedicated to AMT systems, to single systems or to the broader field, one dot per edition; shaded bands mark the eras. The data set was compiled from workshop pages, host conference programs, mailing-list announcements and dblp (retrieved ); only editions that took place and are confirmed by a source are shown. Tap a row for all editions with their sources.

    Swipe sideways to see all years.

    12

    Data

    Citation data from OpenAlex, an open bibliographic database that links the references of the works it indexes (references it cannot match are missing, so some citations are not counted), with Semantic Scholar as a second source for the seed counts only, retrieved . Duplicate records of a seed (reprints, conference and journal versions) are merged; other works with the same title, such as a later tutorial abstract or a book chapter, are not. Counts measure attention, not quality.

    Two databases, one ranking?

    Citing works per seed in OpenAlex (duplicate records merged) against the citation count in Semantic Scholar, on logarithmic axes; the line marks equal counts, and labels mark the seeds whose counts differ by 30% or more (ratio Semantic Scholar to OpenAlex). Hover or tap for all others.

    The BibTeX file holds the curated corpus, all seeds and the further references of the paper; its keywords field marks each entry as seed, corpus, reference or main path (a work on a main path). The JSON data hold everything this page shows; the full field set (every citing work with its OpenAlex ID, DOI, title, year and references) is data/field.json.gz of the reproducibility package.
